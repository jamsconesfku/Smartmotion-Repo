# Business

Summary of *SmartMotion 6DOF Simulator Experiences – Business Plan* (31 Oct 2026, 22 pages). The original PDF goes in `docs/source/` (not uploaded yet).

## Business lines
1. **Experiences (priority, launching 2027).** Paid sessions at the Great Dunmow base, at corporate events, and at airshows and other events. Gift vouchers go on sale first (end of Nov 2026).
2. **Building and selling simulators (parked).** Complete systems (PC and VR installed) to kit packages, "built to suit your budget". Parked until the experiences business is running (task F-01, decision DEC-07).
3. **3D printing (to be defined).** See `docs/3d-printing/`.

## Sales channels
- Bookable sessions at the Dunmow base
- Corporate days (on-site or at the client)
- Walk-up sessions at airshows and events

## Targets
- Revenue about **£13,000/month** within 12 months (base case); profit about £6,400/month before owner pay.
- Break-even about **£3,900/month** (roughly 84 public sessions or 3 corporate days).
- Utilisation 40%, rising to 60–70%.
- Start-up costs about **£9,600–£19,000**, excluding software licences.

## Positioning
- One rig does two jobs: full-motion flight in VR and rally/track driving. Rivals are mostly fixed-base flight sims or 2–3-axis racing rigs.
- Tagline: **"Fly it. Drive it. Feel it."**
- Priority customers: corporate team days and gift-voucher buyers; then aviation enthusiasts, student pilots and events.

## Operations model
Fixed flight days and car days (to avoid repeated conversions), 45-minute slots.

## Market
- **VR Sim**, St Ives, Cambridgeshire (vr-sim.co.uk): nearest competitor. 2 × 4-DOF DCS rigs plus an Assetto Corsa rig. From £20 for 30 minutes mid-week. Recheck our prices against theirs before vouchers go on sale.
- **VA Airline Training**, Cambridge Airport: A320/737 simulators, £360 for 1 hour.
